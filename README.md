# CISSP Study Resources (2026 Edition)

Material and resources for pursuing CISSP (Certified Information Systems Security Professional) certification!

**CISSP Study Resources** is a from-scratch collection of study notes organized by (ISC)²'s official CISSP Certification Exam Outline, current as of September 2026.

## A note on currency

(ISC)² runs its CISSP Job Task Analysis (JTA) on a roughly three-year cycle. The **current outline has been in effect since April 15, 2024** and, as of this writing (September 2026), remains the live outline — CISSP was *not* part of (ISC)²'s 2026 refresh cycle (that cycle updated CCSP, effective August 1, 2026, and the entry-level CC certification, effective September 1, 2026). The 8-domain structure, weightings, and topics below reflect that April 2024 outline, including its expanded coverage of AI/ML security, zero trust, quantum-resistant cryptography, and supply-chain risk — all of which are already native parts of the current CBK, not a future update.

## Overview

- **Who is it for?** CISSP validates deep technical *and* managerial security knowledge — the ability to design, engineer, and manage an organization's overall security posture. Candidates need 5 years of cumulative, paid, full-time experience in 2+ of the 8 domains. A relevant 4-year degree or an approved additional credential can each satisfy up to 1 year of that requirement (only one such waiver applies, not both stacked). Without the required experience, passing the exam earns "Associate of ISC²" status, with 6 years to complete the experience requirement.
- **Think like a manager/owner.** CISSP is written from the perspective of someone accountable for the whole security program, not just a single control. Questions frequently ask for the *best*, *most important*, or *first* action — answer from a risk-based, business-enabling perspective rather than the most technically aggressive option.
- **Exam format:**
  - Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT) for English, German, Spanish-Modern, Japanese, and Simplified Chinese; linear format for other languages
  - 125–150 questions (multiple choice plus "advanced innovative" item types), 3 hours
  - Passing score: 700 out of 1000 points (scaled, not a raw percentage)
- **Domain weights (effective April 15, 2024):**

| Domain | Weight |
|---|---|
| 1. Security and Risk Management | 16% |
| 2. Asset Security | 10% |
| 3. Security Architecture and Engineering | 13% |
| 4. Communication and Network Security | 13% |
| 5. Identity and Access Management (IAM) | 13% |
| 6. Security Assessment and Testing | 12% |
| 7. Security Operations | 13% |
| 8. Software Development Security | 10% |

- **Test-taking strategies**
  - Read the question, then form your own ideal answer *before* reading the options — CAT questions are often written so more than one answer is technically defensible; you're looking for the *best* one.
  - Prioritize answers that reduce risk to the business as a whole over answers that are narrowly, technically "most secure."
  - When stuck, eliminate absolutes and options that describe a process rather than a technology (or vice versa) when the question is clearly asking for the other.
  - Life safety always outranks protecting data, systems, or property in any physical security or incident scenario.
